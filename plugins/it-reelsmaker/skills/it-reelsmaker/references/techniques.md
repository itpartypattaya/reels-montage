# Special techniques

Each one only if the brief chose it (step 7). The cut-out figure itself is in `references/figure.md`; designed scenes are in `references/scenes.md`.

**Verification status.** Not yet verified in a finished video: the light flash and the slide scene; presenter over a scene was tested on a 1 s piece only. The “framed” format was checked on one test render from a simulated horizontal source (a 608-row crop, not a real Zoom recording). The numbers for them are starting values: check stills and the preview with extra care, and say in the plan that the technique is new.

**Cut-out figure**: everything is in `references/figure.md`: `rembg` cut-out with `matte.py` on this computer, or on your own server with the online add-on (frames from `final.mp4` as JPG; the WebM with alpha and edge cleanup; a check frame on a light and a dark background shows furniture in the mask right away), source-edge cuts, layouts.

**Text behind the person (hook).** A big word behind the figure but in front of the background, like a magazine cover. Offer it only if there is no room above the head for the whole hook: with a free “headroom” zone, a regular hook above the head reads better. Needs a calm, contrasting background behind the head; 1.5–3 s. In Remotion, three layers **inside one camera div**: video → word → `<OffthreadVideo src="person.webm" transparent muted />`. The word is ≤ 960 px wide, the figure covers only the bottom or the middle of the letters (≤ ~30%), the lead-in sits tight above the word. **The main risk is contrast:** light letters on a light wall disappear. The “antithesis” variant: the word is crossed out during a pause and replaced by a second word when that word is spoken.

**Presenter over a scene** (overview, review, stream). The speaker is cut out and stands in front of a screen recording, the video being reviewed, or a code scene. Layouts: “review”: the scene in a panel at the top, the presenter's forehead at its bottom edge; “stream”: the scene fills the frame, a small presenter in a corner; “window”: no cut-out, the speaker's video in a rounded window. Scale by the face measurement, side by the source-edge cuts (no chopped-off arm in the frame), separation from the scene by composition, with no outline or shadow.

**“Framed” format** (horizontal source: Zoom, webinar, podcast). Set it in the video's settings: `reelcfg.py save edit/<id> --set format=framed label="CANDIDATE INTERVIEW"` (the label is optional; `window=[x,y,w,h]` moves the window). The kit's `ReelKit` draws it from the props `visual_plan.py export` writes (`props.framed`), with no per-video composition: a window of 1030×1240 at (25, 340), 50 px corners with no feathering, on the style's field (`brand.looks.<style>.field`, else the primary color); the caps label above the window (the brand's body font, 600, 28 px, letter spacing 0.2 em, y ~292–320, in the color that reads on the field); the rough cut as a cover in the window, the virtual camera clamped to the window; scenes, B-roll and the subtitle band inside the window's text area (x 60–960 at the default window, down to y 1500; with no label, the field above the window joins it). Do not scale to 1080×1920 in `cut.py` (`"scale": "auto"` keeps a horizontal source at its size); `faces.py scan` measures it in its source geometry and maps the faces into the window; the camera's `cx, cy` are screen coordinates (`references/camera.md`); change shots when the speaker changes, and shift the frame toward the one who speaks. Scenes are `overlay` (inside the window) or `full` (covering the window); validate refuses `split`, `panel` and `window` here, since the speaker already sits in a window. Cutaways also go inside the window. Don't offer it for a vertical talking head: it only makes the face smaller.

**Zoom margin in the window.** The base shot is the horizontal frame enlarged to the window height: base = window height ÷ source rows, so 1240 / 1080 ≈ ×1.15 for 1080p, 1240 / 720 ≈ ×1.72 for 720p (a typical Zoom recording), 1240 / 608 ≈ ×2.04 for a 608-row crop. The base already uses up the upscaling margin, and the camera's push-in counts on top of it toward the quality ceiling of step 4: 1080p leaves ≈ ×1.13 to the ×1.3 ceiling; at 720p and below the base alone is past it, so no more than ×1.06. A horizontal 1080p source therefore has *less* push-in room than a vertical 1080×1920 one (×1.3): get the dynamics from shifting the frame toward the speaker (`cx`), a light push-in and the scenes.

**Hook in the window.** The window's headroom is often 200–250 px, and two hook lines on plates come out at ~82 px there, under the 92–120 px rule. Two ways: (1) no label: start the hook box on the field above the window (from y 230) and let it run into the window's headroom; the field joins the text area, the hook gets 300 px or more and 92–96 px lines, and it names the topic in place of the label; (2) keep the label and accept a smaller hook inside the window, but not under 76 px (validate warns below 76 px in this format and below 92 px elsewhere). The label never goes inside the window.

**LUT**: see step 6. Start at 50–70 % strength (`lut_mix`; ffmpeg has no skin protection) and compare stills of the face before and after; skin turning orange or gray → lower the strength or fix the correction step (`look.correct`). The correction is measured for every source on the graded result, LUT included: `balance.py edit/<id> --write` (grays neutral within ±2 levels; a LUT that pulls blue down turns the whole video yellow while the face still looks fine), then compare its before/after stills (`verify/balance.png`). A third-party LUT (cinematic, “warm”) only per the brief, also at reduced strength.

**Phone mockup on the CTA.** A PNG phone frame with a transparent screen ⟨YOURS: frame files⟩, with a screen recording or screenshot of where the CTA leads underneath. Compute the screen rectangle from the frame's transparent area (look for the edges not at the center but at a quarter of the width: the notch gets in the way). Frame 480–600 px; entrance: a 40–60 px rise and `rotateY` 10–12° → 0 over 14–18 frames, no “spinning 3D”. Cover other people's personal data in the screenshot. Offer it only when the CTA leads to a concrete place with something to show, not for a generic “follow”. Record a web page at a phone viewport (for example 390×844). The frame sits in a face-free zone (centered on the end card), with `Easing.out(cubic)` and no bounce, and stays no longer than 3–4 s; the screen content may scroll.

**Light flash instead of a transition.** A 6–10-frame flash in `screen` mode at a change of topic blocks, 1–2 per video, within the brand tone's ceiling (none for `premium`, up to 3 for `bold`). Procedurally: a radial warm-white spot with opacity 0 → 0.55 → 0. If the brand forbids light effects, don't use it. The spot sweeps across the frame in ~8 frames, opacity on a sine curve, warm white (for example `rgba(255,236,210,…)`). Only at a change of topic block (hook → body, body → CTA), never on every cut and never on the end card. If the video has sounds, a soft whoosh on it. Check the still at the flash peak: the face is not blown out and the subtitle reads. In the kit this is the `flash` transition of an insert or a scene (the cut itself is hard, under the peak) and `FlashAt` (`kit/LightFlash.tsx`) at any cut of a per-video composition; the warm white is `colors.extra.warm` (or `extra.cream`) of the brand profile, otherwise a kit default; the peak lifts the center of the frame by ~35 levels of 255. An overlay file (flashes on black, `screen` blend, 6–10 frames centered on the cut frame) only with a checked license.

**Slide scene**: covers choppy speech. The speaker shrinks to ~0.42 and slides to the free edge (background in the style color), a panel with the thesis slides in from the other side, points appear on their words. 1–2 times, 3–5 s, the panel no lower than y 1500. Offer it when the take is choppy (many short segments, shot-size changes already used on the neighboring cuts), also for a list of about 3 items on such a take; a list over a clean take goes in `overlay` (the speaker is not moved, `references/scenes.md`). The speaker moves to the side they are already closer to; the shrunken speaker holds a corner of 25–40 % of the frame, with the face not cropped by the frame.


**Full-screen key phrase**: a “punch”, 1–2 times per video. A field in the style color wipes in over 8–10 frames, a pictogram “draws itself”, 3–6 words come up from below one by one on their words. Hide subtitles for that time. Not on the hook and not on the CTA. One style color for the field and no second bright color; the wipe has square corners and no gradient, and leaves by a reverse wipe or a cut. Pictogram 200–300 px, drawn in 10–12 frames (a mask growing top-down). Words at weight 700–800, 80–110 px, left-aligned. Duration: while the phrase is heard, plus 0.3–0.5 s, but never under the reading-time floor (`references/scenes.md`; validate enforces it), and **the floor wins**: a 4-word phrase said in 0.55 s still needs 1.2 s fully on screen plus the entrance and exit, so the scene runs on into the next line and hides its subtitles (a real video: 2.8 s over 1.6 s of the next speech). Decide before adding it: take a phrase of 1–3 words (floor 0.8 s), or accept that the scene covers the start of the next line and say so in the plan (what is hidden, for how long). Sound: one soft hit or paper on the wipe, not on every word.

**Hard cut on silence.** In Remotion, a hard cut is a value that jumps on one frame (`frame >= f(t) ? B : A`), not an `interpolate`. Check: on each transition still, see in `captions.json` whether a word is being spoken at that moment.
